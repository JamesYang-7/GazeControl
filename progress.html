In progress User study design
design settled 2026-08-21, written up in Assets/Docs/user-study-design.md (tracked, beside baseline-spec.md, because it is what the study gets built from). Scene 1 only; scene 2 is discarded from the study for now
  • Within-subjects, 5 conversations × 3 methods = 15 clips per participant, blocked by conversation: the three methods of one conversation play back-to-back, then the participant ranks them. Method order counterbalanced (6 permutations, one per block, rotated across participants); conversation order randomised. N = 30, a multiple of 6 so the counterbalancing closes exactly.
  • Live VR with eye tracking, not video playback — TriadScene ported to XR, the participant standing at the third triad vertex. That is what gives the "seemed aware I was there" item its force: an agent's gaze lands on the participant's own eyes rather than on a camera.
  • Primary DV is the per-block ranking ("rank the three by how natural the characters' *eye movements* were, 1-3, no ties") — immune to scale-use bias and far more sensitive than absolute ratings for a gaze-only difference. Plus four 7-point items per video (gaze naturalness / boundary gaze timing / mutual engagement / being noticed) and a free-text discomfort probe per block.
  • Every item names eye movements, never "behaviour" (user's correction 2026-08-21): body motion is identical across methods, so an item admitting motion is partly rating a constant, which dilutes the effect. Two drafted items were cut for this and for scope — an overall-believability item (admits motion) and a next-speaker-prediction item (contradicts the paper's own position that gaze alone cannot predict turns, and the participant never takes a turn).
  • Eye tracking finally populates mutual_gaze_with_human, the baseline-spec.md §6 column reserved since July but never fillable — the human's gaze was unobservable. Four measures: mutual gaze proportion, gaze allocation on the agent mesh (face vs body heatmap), gaze distribution over the take, and gaze-return latency (when an agent looks at the participant, how long until they look back) — the last is specific to this setup and the most direct evidence that an agent's gaze was read as *directed*.
  • Analysis aggregates to participant level before testing (Friedman + Kendall's *W* on mean ranks; one LMM per Likert construct with participant *and* conversation as random effects). Explicitly not per-clip or per-gaze-sample tests — that error is what produces impressive F values beside effect sizes near 0.0001 in the comparable literature.
  • Open: pilot on 3-5 people before recruiting (session length, whether the framing reads naturally, whether the manipulation is perceived at all); formal power calculation to confirm N = 30.

Decisions 127

  • 2026-08-26 (latest) — The study's seeds are unselected and recorded, not chosen from the middle of a scan (user's call, after asking what would actually be wrong with the arbitrary ones). They are right that this is the stronger position: fairness requires only that the choice carry no information about the resulting take, and it demonstrably did not — the broken scan's numbers correlate with the real ones at r = +0.20. So the original seeds (8/4/7/7/4) were a legitimate arbitrary draw, and "the base seed was fixed arbitrarily and recorded" is a sentence that needs no defending, unlike a selection rule.
  • 2026-08-26 (latest) — Baseline B is not bit-reproducible across bakes; the other two conditions are. Re-baking all five clips at identical seeds reproduced four exactly and moved study_c4 by 19 ticks (0.32 s). Its input is the audio playback position, scheduled against the DSP clock, so *when the first voice onset is confirmed* can land either side of the decision grid — it changes only how long the agents watch the participant before anyone has spoken. Harmless for a study session, since participants replay the file, but "a bake is regenerable" holds bit-for-bit only for the schedule-driven conditions.
  • 2026-08-26 (later) — The decision tick is 60 Hz, not 30 (user's question, then their call). Nothing had ever justified 30: baseline-spec.md §0.2 said "20-50 Hz is plenty" and argued only for *decoupling* decisions from the frame rate. Both data sources are 60 fps, so 60 Hz is one tick per recorded frame — the prototypes are 1 s windows of 60 frames and now map 1:1, and the holding corpus's fixation durations are exact multiples of 1/60 s. Measured over the bank's 7,150 durations: mean rounding error 8.17 ms at 30 Hz against 0.02 ms at 60. HoldingReplayGazePolicy already carried a workaround comment for that quantisation. Cost is 2× ticks (~1,400 samples per agent per clip) and it re-opens every seed, which is why it was settled before baking rather than after.
  • 2026-08-26 (later) — Baseline B could never be baked or replayed, and nobody had noticed. Found by the user watching a bake: agent B stared at the participant for the whole take. One cause, two symptoms. TryPrepareCondition returned early for SpeakerFollowing — it needs no director — before the block that allocates the bake recorders and loads a replay track. So baking baseline B threw NullReferenceException on every tick and wrote no file, and because the throw happened at *agent A's* recorder line it aborted the tick before agent B was reached, leaving agent B on its Awake-initialised target (the human) forever. Replay was equally broken: _track was never loaded for it. All three study conditions could not have run from tracks.
  • 2026-08-26 (latest) — The seed scan was sweeping every seed through one director that cannot rewind. ConversationDirector.SyncToClock only ever walks _index *forward* — correct for a live conversation, which never goes back — but SeedScan.Run reused a single director across all twelve seeds and reset only the clock. So the first seed scanned was right and every later one ran the entire simulation against a schedule frozen on the final turn. That is the whole discrepancy: yesterday's cand_v008 check passed because it scanned a *single* seed, one forward sweep; the twelve-seed scans used to pick the study clips were reading frozen roles for eleven of twelve seeds.
  • 2026-08-26 (later) — ~~The seed scan is not a predictor and the docs said it was.~~ Cause found and fixed the same day — see the entry above. Recorded because the measurement stands: Over the five study clips it missed the baked aversion fraction by up to 25 percentage points (study_c1 agent B: predicted 52%, baked 26.8%; study_c5 agent A: predicted 31%, baked 50.3%), having matched cand_v008 exactly on 2026-08-25. SeedScan.BuildState assembles its own ConversationState rather than the runner's — partners from Speakers with a User fallback instead of the triad, MutualGazeActive hard-coded false — and one different early draw cascades, because holding stretches average ~4 s. Cause not isolated. §10 now says a bake is the ground truth and a scan only ranks seeds; the claim that they "agree exactly" is withdrawn.
